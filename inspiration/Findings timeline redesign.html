What changed

  1. No text inside the plot. Agent names and their live status (Idle, Running, Waiting) moved to a fixed gutter on the left.
  2. Short finding markers. Findings get their own track above the lanes. A short dotted link ties each marker to the run it started, replacing the full-height lines.
  3. A gap for the handoff. The lanes are pulled apart. An amber arrow drops when the security agent calls out, a violet arrow rises when the external agent replies, and a soft band spans the round trip.
  4. Blocked vs working. While it waits for the reply, the caller’s bar turns hatched, so you can see who is actually busy.
  5. Outcome lives in the label. “Dismissed in 1s” moved from the plot into the finding’s label.
  6. Details on demand. Hovering anything highlights the whole chain for that finding and fills the line under the axis.

The two label options

  • Sticky callouts. The label rides with its marker and its caret always points straight at it. At the plot edges, or when labels would collide, the label stops and the caret slides along its edge instead.
  • Fixed labels + pins. Closest to your idea: labels never move. They stack from the right, newest first, and a numbered pin travels with each marker. Order is preserved, so pins and labels read in the same sequence.

Assumptions in this mock

  • The dashed ticks at the bottom of your chart are scans; they now sit in their own strip.
  • Each finding starts one security-agent run, and a run makes at most one external call.
  • The window is a rolling two minutes.
